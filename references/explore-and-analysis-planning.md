## Inputs

Prefer these inputs when available:

- data file or variable listing;
- data guide, codebook, questionnaire, or source methodology;
- user's target population or rough research question;
- outcome, exposure, mediator, moderator, and covariate ideas;
- design information for weights or sampling when applicable.

Do not require all inputs before doing useful orientation work.

## Dataset Exploration

1. Identify data source, wave/year, target population, and unit of analysis.
2. Scan variable names, labels, codebook definitions, missing codes, and survey
   design fields.
3. Identify variables that can be used directly, variables requiring
   preparation, and concepts that are not measured well enough.
4. Distinguish association questions, prediction questions, descriptive
   comparisons, matching questions, and survey population-estimate questions.
5. Suggest the smallest defensible next analysis before proposing complex
   models.

Use an analysis-plan table when multiple questions compete:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Add a short list of `Preparation needed before analysis` when derived variables,
reverse coding, eligibility filters, or score validation are required.

## Feasibility Cautions

- Do not call a variable a mediator merely because it lies between two concepts
  rhetorically. Check theory, measurement order, and study design.
- For secondary data, identify missing constructs and measurement limitations
  early instead of promising a model the data cannot support.
- When survey design is present or likely, state whether the current output is a
  rough unweighted exploration or a population-facing survey analysis.
